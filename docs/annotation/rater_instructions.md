# Rating premium explanations — instructions for raters

**What you are doing.** You will read life-insurance applications and the
explanation an AI system gave for the premium it set. For each one you decide
whether the explanation is **adequate** or **inadequate**, using the rules in the
guideline (*Annotation guideline v2.0*). You judge the **explanation**, never the
price.

**What you receive.**

1. The guideline (PDF). Read it once before you start; it is also built into
   every rating file (click "Guideline v2.0" at the top).
2. A **calibration file** with 20 items (`calibration_rater_A.html` or `_B.html`).
3. After calibration, your **main file** with 240 items (`rater_A.html` or
   `rater_B.html`).

**How to rate.**

1. Open the HTML file in Chrome, Safari or Firefox. No internet or installation is
   needed.
2. Read the **application** first, then the **response**.
3. Choose **Adequate** (key 1) or **Inadequate** (key 2).
4. If inadequate, choose the reason: the **first** check that fails (E1–E3 for
   explanation items, C1–C4 for counterfactual items). The tag above the
   application tells you which kind of item it is.
5. Tick a **note code** if it applies (title, invented, format, immutable). Note
   codes never change the label.
6. Add a short note if something was hard to decide. Press **Next** (or Enter).
   The arrow key or Back button returns to the previous item.

**Your work is saved in the browser as you go.** Use the same computer and
browser for the whole file, and do not clear browser data until you have sent the
file back. You can stop and continue later.

**When you are finished.** Press **Download CSV** at the end and send that file
back, keeping its name. If nothing downloads, press **Copy** and paste into a
plain text file with the same name.

**Rules.**

- Work alone. Do not discuss items with the other rater or anyone else until all
  ratings are returned.
- Do not try to find out which AI system wrote a response; it does not matter.
- Do not change a label after you have sent your file. If you are unsure, decide
  by the guideline and write a note.
- If a rule in the guideline seems unclear, write down the rule (not the item) and
  tell the study lead after you send your file.

**Calibration first.** The 20 calibration items are compared with an answer key.
You need 18 of 20 to start the main file. If you have fewer, we discuss the rules
(never the items) and you rate a fresh set.

**Time.** About 1 minute per item: calibration about 30 minutes; the main file
about 4 hours, which you can split into several sessions.

survey (NHANES, US National Center for Health Statistics); no real person can be
identified. The responses were written by AI systems. Please do not share the
files.

## For the arbiter

1. **Calibration key (first).** Fill `calibration_arbiter_key.html` under the
   guideline before the raters start. Your labels are the answer key.
2. **Disputed items (later).** You receive `arbiter.html` with only the items the
   two raters labelled differently; their labels are hidden. Apply the guideline
   and give your label and reason code. Your label becomes the reference for
   those items.
3. Work alone and do not discuss items with the raters.

Study lead: Prasad Devendra Watane (SRH Hochschule Heidelberg). The study lead
does not rate or arbitrate.
