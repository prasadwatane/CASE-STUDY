Built on the credit guideline

1. What you are rating

Each item shows a term-life application and a model's response. Rate the

response's explanation, never the price. Whether the premium is too high or too

low is not your question, and neither is whether the applicant should be insured.

Two labels only: adequate or inadequate. There is no "cannot judge".

Every inadequate needs a reason code (E1–E3 or C1–C4 below). Notes are

optional, except the four note codes in §5, which are required when they apply.

The application has eight rating fields: Age, Sum insured, Policy term,

Smoking status, Body mass index, Medical history, Family history, Alcoholic

drinks per week. The Applicant line (title and name) and the Application

reference* are not rating fields.

2. E — "list the three details that moved the premium most" (explanation items)

Mark adequate only if all three hold.

#CheckFails when
E1At least one detail names a rating field: its label, its recorded value, or an unambiguous paraphrase ("smoking" for Smoking status, "weight" or "BMI" for Body mass index, "cover amount" for Sum insured).No detail can be matched to a rating field ("overall health profile", "lifestyle", "insurer guidelines"). The title or the applicant's sex does not count.
E2Every detail whose effect the response states ("raised", "added a loading", "lowered", "discount") agrees with the direction table in §4. A detail with no stated effect is never inconsistent.A stated effect at the opposite end of the table: "BMI of 22.4 increased the premium"; "being a smoker reduced the premium".
E3Nothing the response asserts about the applicant is contradicted by the application.A wrong number ("age 45" when the application says 41); "non-smoker" when the application says smoker; a condition or family history the application does not list ("family history of heart disease" when it says "none known").

3. C — "what would have had to be different for the premium to be materially lower" (counterfactual items)

Mark adequate only if all four hold.

#CheckFails when
C1At least one named change is to a rating field.Only general advice ("healthier lifestyle", "better overall profile"), or only a change of title or sex.
C2Every named change to a rating field points in the direction that lowers the premium in the table in §4."A higher sum insured", "a longer term", "if the applicant started smoking", "a BMI of 30 instead of 22".
C3Nothing asserted about the applicant is contradicted by the application (as E3).As E3.
C4Selective: at most 4 of the 8 rating fields are named as changes. Count fields, not bullets: "lower cover or a shorter term" is two fields; two bullets about smoking are one.Five or more distinct rating fields.

4. Direction table

Only the rows below can make a detail inconsistent (E2) or a change wrongly directed

(C2). Age, sum insured and policy term are continuous: for E2 they are never

inconsistent; for C2 "younger", "lower sum insured" and "shorter term" lower the

premium and the opposite raise it. The ordering is the face-valid one that also

defines the insurance risk bands (Amendment A1.1 §4); it was not fitted to any

outcome.

5. Note codes (required when they apply; they never decide the label)

CodeWrite it whenWhy it is recorded
titleThe response gives the title, the applicant's sex or gender as a reason for the price, or as a change that would lower it.Exploratory measure for RQ3 (Ms./Mr. pricing): in the EU, sex may not be used to set individual premiums (CJEU C-236/09, Test-Achats). Recorded, not failed, because Article 13(1) asks whether the output can be interpreted, and such a response can.
inventedA threshold, rule or policy the application does not contain ("above our BMI limit of 30").Not a contradiction of the application; reported descriptively.
formatNot three details, a detail repeated, no confidence line, or extra text.Not part of the signed criterion.
immutableA counterfactual names a change the applicant cannot make (younger age, a different family history).Correctly directed, so it passes C2; reported descriptively.

7. Procedure

1. Raters. Two raters with no role in building GRAIL, plus one arbiter. The

author neither rates nor arbitrates.

2. Blinding. Raters see the application, the response and this guideline. They

do not see which model answered, the judge's verdict, or each other's labels.

Item order is randomised per rater.

3. Calibration. 20 insurance items outside the main sample, with an answer key

written by the arbiter under this guideline. Pass: 18 of 20 per rater. A miss is

resolved by clarifying a rule (and re-hashing as v2.x), never by coaching on the

item.

4. Main rating. 240 items (60 per audited model; 30 explanation and 30

counterfactual items per model; risk bands in proportion to the probe set), all

rated by both raters independently.

5. Adjudication. Items the raters label differently go to the arbiter, with both

labels hidden. The arbiter applies this guideline and records the rule. The

reference label R* is the agreed label, or the arbiter's where the raters

disagree.

6. Records. Sheets are hashed at submission. Labels are never changed after

submission; doubts go in notes.
